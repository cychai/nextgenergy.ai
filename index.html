Approach

Three ideas that run through every project.

They are not product features. They are how we decide what to build, what to test, and what to write down.

01

The thermal path has six boundaries, and no single vendor owns them all.

Cold plate, quick disconnect, manifold, CDU, facility loop, heat rejection. A leak, a fouled plate or a lost megawatt of reuse always happens at a boundary. Someone has to own the whole line.

Read →02

Return-water grade is the first thing that decides whether your waste heat has a buyer.

Not the PUE. The temperature the loop returns at is the first screen for who can take the heat directly: a greenhouse, a district network, a pool, an absorption chiller. Load profile, distance and contract terms come next.

Read →03

A leak is three different failures on three different clocks.

A weld that escaped the factory. A quick disconnect disturbed at commissioning. A hose that ages out in year four. Different owners, different evidence. One checkbox cannot cover them.

Numbers with boundaries

A number goes out with its condition attached, or it does not go out.

Every performance figure we publish about our own work carries its test condition and its source. Reference figures and calculator assumptions carry a stated basis and a stated limit. A figure we cannot yet state that way does not appear as a headline.

≈55 °CCold-plate loops we have delivered return water at roughly 55 °C.Condition: Secondary-loop return at rated IT load; direct-to-chip cold plate; facility-side approach not included. Source: Every Watt Counts, "Waste Heat Becomes an Asset" (June 2026). Verified by: Operating logs; not third-party audited.
50–70 °CFourth-generation low-temperature district heating networks typically accept supply in the 50–70 °C band.Condition: Varies by network, season and building stock; retrofit networks sit at the top of the band. Source: Published 4GDH literature; summarised in Every Watt Counts, "Waste Heat Becomes an Asset".
−40 °COur cold-climate designs are specified for ambient down to −40 °C.Condition: Design ambient for outdoor heat-rejection equipment and fluid selection; not a demonstrated continuous-operation record at that temperature. Source: NextGenergy design basis for cold-climate deployments.
