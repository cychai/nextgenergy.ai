Who can buy your heat?

Enter the temperature your loop returns at and the heat you have. The tool compares the temperature you can deliver, after the heat exchanger, with the supply window each class of off-taker typically wants, and tells you whether the temperature matches, whether a heat-pump lift would be needed, or whether it falls outside the screening range. Temperature is the first screen, not a feasibility verdict.

Off-takers, by physical feasibility
What this tool does and does not do. It screens on delivered temperature only. Real feasibility also depends on the buyer's load profile and season, distance and pumping cost, availability and back-up, hydraulic design, and the commercial terms. Outputs are illustrative screening estimates, not equipment selections, guarantees or investment-grade forecasts.
Method and assumptions
  • Delivered temperature = return-water temperature − heat-exchanger approach you enter.
  • Temperature match: delivered temperature is at or above the lower edge of the off-taker's indicative supply window. Lift needed: within 25 K below it. Outside screening range: more than 25 K below it.
  • Heat-pump lift: idealised, lift-only estimate at 50 % of Carnot between delivered temperature and the window's lower edge, capped at COP 8. It ignores exchanger approaches on the heat-pump side, part load, auxiliaries and seasonal performance.
  • Water flow = heat ÷ (4.18 kJ/kg·K × ΔT), water properties at ~50 °C.
  • Annual heat = continuous heat × 8760 h, i.e. full year-round off-take.
  • Windows are indicative supply temperatures drawn from published engineering practice for each class: legacy 2nd/3rd-generation district heating, single-effect LiBr absorption chillers (many machines need more than 70 °C; check the maker's curve), industrial wash and pre-heat duties, 4th-generation district heating (some ultra-low-temperature networks run below 45 °C), greenhouse pipe-rail heating, aquaculture (species-specific), low-temperature building emitters, pools and domestic-hot-water pre-heat (final DHW temperature and legionella control need a separate top-up). Verify against the actual buyer's system.
